SEQ Transit AI

Delay prediction for the Gold Coast to Brisbane corridor
Try itCodeWrite-up
THE PROBLEM

TransLink publishes system-wide monthly on-time averages, but no stop-level or per-line delay history. Journey planners show the timetable. None of them say how much to trust it, or when you should actually leave.

WHAT I BUILT

I built the sensor first. An archiver on EC2 polls TransLink's GTFS-Realtime feeds every five minutes and stores them in S3, including the tram endpoints the combined feed leaves out. That gave me 156 million rows across 46 days. An XGBoost model trained on that archive predicts arrival delay. The live app takes a From and To stop and returns direct or multi-leg trips across bus, rail and tram, a leave-by time, and a High, Medium or Low confidence label.

THE DECISION THAT MATTERED

When the model meets a stop it hasn't seen in training, it forces the confidence label to Low, even when the prediction looks clean. A wrong answer that looks confident is worse than no answer.

RESULT

Test MAE of 1.957 minutes against a 2.486-minute naive baseline, a 21.3% improvement. Live on AWS (EC2, S3, CloudFront) with a FastAPI back end and a Leaflet map.

LIMITS AND NEXT

Tram data is still thin, so tram predictions carry lower confidence. Live delay isn't blended into the production prediction yet; that's the next model version. Ferry is excluded on purpose.

Automated Scheme Research Tool

A retrieval-augmented assistant for government scheme pages
CodeWrite-up
THE PROBLEM

Government schemes go unused because the details sit in long pages nobody reads: who is eligible, what you get, how to apply, which documents you need.

WHAT I BUILT

A retrieval-augmented assistant. You give it scheme URLs or a file of URLs. LangChain loads the pages, SentenceTransformers embeds them, and a FAISS index finds the passage closest to your question. DistilBERT extracts the answer from that passage, and the Streamlit chat shows the answer with its source URL and a short summary. The index is saved to disk, so the same pages aren't processed twice.

THE DECISION THAT MATTERED

It runs on free Hugging Face models. A tool that only works with a paid API key isn't much use to the organisations that need it most.

RESULT

A working end-to-end RAG pipeline that cites its source for every answer. The same pattern applies to internal policy and procedure documents.

LIMITS AND NEXT

It retrieves a single best passage per question, and the summary is a short extract from that passage. Next: categorise schemes by topic and update the index as new URLs are added.

PythonLangChainHugging Face SentenceTransformersFAISSDistilBERTStreamlit

Maternal Health and Early Outbreak Warning

A prototype risk screen and outbreak dashboard for low-resource clinics
CodeWrite-up
THE PROBLEM

Prompted by the UNCTAD white paper on AI for Good: low-resource clinics need to spot high-risk pregnancies and unusual symptom spikes early, on modest hardware.

WHAT I BUILT

A prototype with two parts. A maternal risk screen takes age, blood pressure, haemoglobin, gestational age and prior history, and returns a risk score. An outbreak dashboard tracks reported fever, cough and diarrhoea by location and flags unusual spikes. Streamlit front end, SQLite behind it.

THE DECISION THAT MATTERED

Logistic regression and z-score anomaly detection, nothing more sophisticated. A health worker has to be able to understand why a case was flagged, and it has to run on a laptop or low-cost cloud.

RESULT

A working, documented prototype trained and tested on synthetic data.

LIMITS AND NEXT

It's a prototype. It isn't a medical device and hasn't been clinically validated. Encryption, hashed identifiers and bias testing across groups are listed as prerequisites before anyone takes it further. Being explicit about that boundary mattered more than the model did.
